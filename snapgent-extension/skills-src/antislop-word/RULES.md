Writing rules (antislop). Condensed from antislop-copywriting by Miqdad Badjuber, MIT License, https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop (commit 35b54dc).

Apply these rules to every piece of text you write into the document. The goal is prose that reads like a person wrote it.

Never invent facts. Do not add any number, name, date, quote, citation or claim that is not in the user's material. If a detail is missing, ask for it or write the plain version without it.

Avoid these patterns:
1. Empty AI vocabulary: unlock, elevate, empower, delve, showcase, testament, journey, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionary, landscape (abstract). Say the concrete thing instead.
2. Significance inflation: 'a pivotal moment', 'a new era', 'the future of', 'a testament to'.
3. Weasel attributions: 'experts say', 'studies show', 'many believe' with no named source. Name the source or cut the sentence.
4. Persuasive authority tropes: 'at its core', 'what really matters', 'the real question is', 'fundamentally'.
5. Chatbot leftovers: 'I hope this helps', 'Let me know if you have questions', 'Certainly!'. They do not belong in a document.
6. Fake-candid openers and signposting: 'Honestly?', 'Here's the thing', 'Let's dive in', 'Here's what you need to know', 'In this section we will explore'. Start with the subject.
7. Temporal runway openers: 'In today's fast-paced world', 'In an ever-evolving landscape'. Delete them.
8. Forced rhythm: lists of three when the content has two or four items, 'not just X, but Y', runs of short dramatic fragments ('No rules. No limits. No fear.'), theatrical lines like 'Read that again' or 'Let that sink in', aphorism formulas ('X is the language of Y'), false ranges ('from A to Z and everything in between').
9. Synonym cycling: repeat the clearest word instead of rotating synonyms.
10. Generic positive endings: 'The future looks bright', 'Exciting times lie ahead'. End on the last concrete point.
11. Actorless passive and agentless abstractions: prefer 'We rewrote the page' over 'The page was rewritten', and avoid 'the data tells us' or 'the tool understands'. Passive is fine when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
12. Filler and stacked hedging: 'in order to' (use 'to'), 'due to the fact that' (use 'because'), 'it is important to note that' (cut), 'could potentially possibly' (use one qualifier).

Formatting hygiene:
- Do not use em dashes or ' -- '. Use a period, a comma, a colon or parentheses instead.
- Do not write whole sentences in ALL CAPS for emphasis.
- Do not bold every key term. Do not scatter quotation marks around ordinary words.
- Do not start list items with a bolded label and a colon when the label only repeats the item.
- Use sentence case in list items, not Title Case On Every Word.
- Do not put decorative emojis in headings or bullets.

Keep the human voice:
- Vary sentence length. Mix short and long sentences.
- Keep specific, unusual details the user supplied. Do not round them off.
- If the user gives a writing sample, match its tone and habits before applying these rules.
- Do not over-sanitize. Text with no voice is as obviously machine-made as text full of the patterns above.
- Look for clusters of patterns, not one isolated instance. A single formal word or one em dash in the user's own text is not a reason to rewrite it.
- Leave quotations, titles, proper names and quoted examples unchanged.

Before delivering, check: no invented facts, none of the patterns above, no em dashes, sentences name who does what, and it reads naturally aloud.
